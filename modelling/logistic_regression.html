Binary logistic regression

Assignments

Sociologists often claim that labor market outcomes (e.g., income, promotion, reentry after unemployment, …) depend not only on human capital factors (formal qualifications, labor market experience, …), but also on social capital: it is not only what you know, but also who you know.

Use the dataset unemploy. Construct the outcome work, coded 1 (or TRUE) if studytim (the unemployment spell length) is less than 53 (weeks), and 0 (or FALSE) otherwise. Human capital is captured by edu (education), age, and exp (number of previous unemployment spells).

  1. Fit a linear probability model of work on age, edu, and exp and interpret all coefficients.

  2. Compute heteroskedasticity-consistent (robust) standard errors for the previous models. You can do that using modelsummary(your_model_object, vcov = "robust"). Are the substantive conclusions affected?

  3. Obtain the predicted probabilities from the linear probability model (use the predict() from base R, or the values from your_model_object$fitted.values). Count how many predicted probabilities fall outside the range 0 to 1.

  4. Fit a logistic regression of work on age, edu, and exp. (Use glm, which has the basically the same syntax as lm(). But you should specify the argument family = binomial().) Interpret each coefficient:

  1. in terms of log-odds
  2. in terms of odds-ratios/odds
  3. in terms of marginal effects (on the probability scale). You can compute marginal effects using marginaleffects::avg_slopes(). Compare these effects, and the substantive conclusions they imply, to the coefficients of the linear probability model from question 1.

  1. Compute the marginal effect of age for a person with the lowest education level (edu = 1) and the largest number of prior unemployment spells (exp = 3). Use marginaleffects::slopes() with the newdata argument to specify the values of edu and exp (see example here). Interpret this effect.

  2. Compute the marginal effect of age at the means of edu and exp. (Use newdata = "mean" in marginaleffects::slopes().) Compare this to the average marginal effect of age found in question 4c. Why can these two values differ?

  3. The dataset comprises measures for three forms of social recourses (strong, medium, and weak ties): scweak, scmedium, and scstrong. Fit a logit model of work on age, edu, exp, and the three social capital measures.

  1. Test, using a Wald test and a likelihood ratio test, whether social capital as a set significantly contributes to explaining re-employment.
  2. Test whether the coefficients for scweak and scstrong are different. (Hint: use marginaleffects::hypotheses())
  1. Fit a probit regression of work on age, edu, and exp. (The code is almost the same as for the logit model, but with family = binomial(link = "probit")) Compare the estimates of this model with the estimates of the linear probability model question 1 and the average marginal effects using the logistic regression model estimated in 4c. What do you find?

The odds of getting a job within a year (the probability of getting a job divided by the probability of not getting a job) change by (exp(b) - 1) * 100% for a one-unit increase in x: - the odds increase by (exp(0.259) - 1) * 100 = 29.5% for one unit of education - the odds change by (exp(-0.143) - 1) * 100 = -13.3%, i.e. decrease by 13.3%, for each year older - the odds decrease by about 19.5% for each previous unemployment spell (exp(-0.216)) The advantage of the odds ratio interpretation is that it applies exactly for every subject, irrespective of the value of the focal variable and of the values of the other predictors – unlike a marginal effect, which depends on the specific values of all predictors.
